## What Changed in This Version

The first version of this plan was built on strategy and instinct. This version is built on the data from the Growth Diagnostic. The core story and the ecosystem are the same — but the sequencing, the ICP, the channel load, and the lead message have all been tightened. Here's the short version of what changed and why:

| What changed | Why it changed |
|---|---|
| Lead message is now the restaurant anchor story — not the founder story | The chef who puts Phlippens on the menu BY NAME is the most powerful social proof available. It's third-party validation from a trusted authority. The founder story supports it, not the other way around. |
| ICP narrowed to KW specialty retail + food service for 90 days | 50+ accounts are already reordering. The diagnostic confirmed the gap is activation (getting more out of who's already in) not acquisition. New channels open after the foundation is proven. |
| Channel load reduced to 3 active channels max | Two people. One is a founder, one is a consultant. Nine channels is a recipe for mediocre at everything. |
| Campaign resequenced to Layer 1 → 4 order | Foundation (unit economics, brand anchor) must be solid before strategy (channel mix) and strategy before execution (content calendar). The diagnostic made this clear. |
| DTC/Klaviyo lane added | Content-to-purchase path was missing. Recipe content needs to flow directly to purchase. |
| Existing retail accounts reframed as the primary audience | These 50+ accounts are the campaign's best asset. Activating them (reorder rate, display, word of mouth) is higher ROI than finding new locations. |
| Bundle pricing referenced throughout | $34.99 four-pack, $99.99 twelve-pack are the DTC purchase points — they need to anchor all DTC content. |

## Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** Take the Lid Off

**One-sentence summary:** A 3-month focused campaign that deepens Phlippens' roots in Waterloo Region — activating existing retail accounts, building the restaurant anchor story into the brand's primary proof point, and connecting product to community through the Men Who Walk & Talk movement.

**Primary objective:** Turn existing momentum (50+ reordering accounts, a restaurant chef who uses the product by name) into a visible, media-ready brand story that compounds over 90 days.

**Secondary objectives:**
- Activate existing retail accounts — increase reorder frequency and in-store visibility
- Build the restaurant anchor story into the brand's most shareable content asset
- Place Phlippens product at the KW Suicide Prevention golf tournament
- Start and grow an email list (start collecting from day one, even from existing customers)
- Open the DTC content-to-purchase lane via recipe content + Klaviyo

**Budget:** Bootstrapped — $0–500/month (time rich, cash lean)
**Timeline:** 3-month sprint
**Geography:** Waterloo Region, Ontario (primary)
**Team:** Two people. Kris (founder) + Evan (strategy/consultant). Everything must be achievable with that.

## ICP: Who This 90 Days Is For

**This campaign does NOT try to reach everyone.**

### Primary ICP — Existing Specialty Retail Accounts (KW)
The 50+ accounts already reordering are the highest-leverage audience in the entire ecosystem. The job here is activation, not acquisition:
- Get product off the shelf and into featured positions (end caps, counter displays, signage)
- Give account owners a story to tell — the restaurant chef angle is it
- Increase reorder frequency by making the product easier to talk about
- Turn account owners into brand advocates, not just stockists

### Secondary ICP — Food Service / Restaurant Buyers (KW)
One chef is already using Phlippens by name on the menu. That's proof of concept for a food service lane. The 90-day job is to document that story and use it to open 2–3 more food service conversations, not to build a full food service channel.

### Tertiary ICP — The Man Who's Almost Ready (MWWT)
Men 28–50 in Waterloo Region who carry more than they show. He'd never sign up for a men's mental health group, but would walk with a group of blokes. He buys interesting things at farmers markets. He'd share a jar of Phlippens at a BBQ without calling it anything. This audience is served through MWWT content and the golf tournament — not through paid acquisition.

**What's explicitly NOT in scope for 90 days:** New retail channel development outside KW, national distribution conversations, mass consumer advertising, platform expansion beyond 3 channels.

## The Lead Message: The Restaurant Anchor Story

The most powerful single asset in the Phlippens brand right now is not the founder story. It's the chef who put Phlippens on the menu by name.

When a chef with a reputation features an ingredient by name, it signals:
- Professional-grade quality (chefs don't risk their menu on mediocre products)
- Versatility at a high level (it works in a kitchen, not just on a patio)
- Community connection (a local chef choosing a local product)

**The restaurant anchor story becomes the lead message in:**
- Every sampling conversation at retail ("This is what a local chef is using by name")
- The golf tournament presence ("The sauce a KW chef put on the menu")
- Every media pitch ("Local condiment brand featured by name on restaurant menu")
- The first Keep It Phlippen Real long-form episode
- The DTC email sequence

The founder story (Kris, hand-smoking, Ontario-made) is the SUPPORTING story. It comes second, not first.

## Layer 1: Foundation (Weeks 1–2)
*Do this before any public-facing execution*

This is the work that doesn't show on Instagram but makes everything else work better.

### Unit Economics Check
Before spending a dollar on sampling or events, confirm the numbers:
- Cost per jar (COGS)
- Retail margin at current price points
- DTC margins at $34.99 four-pack and $99.99 twelve-pack
- Break-even sampling volume (how many samples before a reorder pays for them)

If the unit economics aren't solid, fix them before scaling. The BDC financing conversation becomes easier when the numbers are clear. Note: if capital is needed to scale beyond bootstrapped pace, the BDC angle (as identified in the diagnostic) is worth a proper conversation — but only after unit economics are documented.

### Brand Anchor: Film the Restaurant Story
**This is the most important production task in the campaign.** Before anything else:
- Get 20–30 minutes with the chef on camera
- Let them explain, in their own words, why they use Phlippens and what it does on the plate
- Film in the kitchen — action is better than interview
- This footage becomes: the hero Reel, the first KIPR episode, the media pitch attachment, the retail conversation leave-behind

Phone is fine. Natural kitchen light is fine. Authenticity beats production value here.

### Klaviyo Setup (DTC lane)
- Create a free Klaviyo account if not already done
- Set up one welcome sequence (3 emails max):
  - Email 1: The restaurant story — "A KW chef is using this. Here's why."
  - Email 2: How to use it (recipe content) — link to online store
  - Email 3: The MWWT connection — "Food sparks community. Here's what we're building."
- Add sign-up link to both Instagram bios and the online store

### Existing Account Audit
List all 50+ reordering accounts. For each:
- Last order date
- Order frequency
- Current in-store placement (shelf vs. counter vs. hidden)
- Owner's name and relationship quality

Identify the top 10 accounts by order frequency + placement quality. These are the activation targets.

## Layer 2: Strategy (Weeks 2–4)

### Channel Focus: Three Channels, Done Well

**The two-person bandwidth reality:** Kris is a founder running production, sales, and delivery. Evan is a consultant with multiple clients. Nine channels will produce mediocre content on all of them. Three channels, done consistently, will compound.

**The three channels for this 90 days:**

**1. Instagram (primary reach + story)**
Single focus per account. Phlippens leads with product in context — especially the restaurant story and real table moments. MWWT leads with trail photography and walk testimonials. 3 posts/week combined is achievable. One Reel per week minimum — Reels drive reach, carousels drive saves.

**2. Facebook Events (walk and tournament activation)**
Every MWWT walk gets a Facebook Event. The golf tournament gets a Facebook Event with KW Suicide Prevention co-listed. This is not a content channel — it's a logistics and invitation channel. Low time cost, high local impact.

**3. Email (Klaviyo — DTC lane and list building)**
One email per month minimum. The list is built from: sampling events, walk sign-ups, Instagram bio link, in-store QR cards at existing retail accounts. The email list is the only channel Phlippens owns. Start now, even with 20 subscribers.

**What's explicitly deprioritized:** TikTok (not enough bandwidth), LinkedIn (wrong audience for this phase), paid ads (not yet — organic story must be proven first), Twitter/X (irrelevant for this audience).

### Existing Account Activation Strategy
The 50+ accounts are the campaign's best distribution asset. The activation job is:
- Give every account owner a copy of the restaurant anchor story (one-page or card)
- Ask the top 10 accounts for better placement (counter, end cap, or signage)
- Leave a QR card that takes customers from the product to the email sign-up or KIPR content
- Check in monthly — not to upsell, but to hear what customers are saying

**This is a relationship activation, not a sales call.**

### DTC Content-to-Purchase Lane
Recipe content is the engine. The sequence:
1. Recipe Reel (Instagram) — shows the product in use, real kitchen, no styling
2. Caption includes a "link in bio" cue
3. Bio link goes to a landing page with the recipe + a buy button (four-pack at $34.99)
4. Purchase triggers Klaviyo welcome sequence
5. Welcome sequence deepens the brand story and drives repeat

Every recipe post needs a purchase path. If there's no easy path from "that looks good" to "I bought it," the content is working for awareness but not revenue.

## Layer 3: Centrepiece Activation — Golf Tournament × KW Suicide Prevention

This is the most strategically important moment in the 3-month window.

**Why it works:** The golf tournament compresses the entire ecosystem into one afternoon. Men, outside, together, for a cause that fights the villain directly. Phlippens shows up with food. MWWT has a presence. Phlippen the Script films the story. It's not a sponsorship activation — it's proof that the ecosystem is real.

### What to Bring / Do

**Phlippens:**
- Sampling station at the 19th hole or event dinner
- Lead with the restaurant anchor story ("A KW chef uses this on the menu. Try it and see why.")
- Jars as prizes in a hole challenge or raffle
- Every sample comes with a story card: one side tells the restaurant story, other side has QR code linking to the KIPR video or the next MWWT walk

**Men Who Walk & Talk:**
- Table or banner presence (not a booth — a presence)
- Simple sign-up sheet or QR code for the next walk
- One person (Kris or Evan) speaks briefly if possible — 60 seconds, honest, about why they walk and who it's for

**Keep It Phlippen Real:**
- Film the day — phone is fine, honest footage, real conversations
- One longer edit (3–5 min) and 3–5 short clips for Reels
- This becomes the campaign's best-performing content

**KW Suicide Prevention:**
- Confirm co-promotion before the event, not after
- Ask them to cross-post and tag — their audience becomes the campaign's audience for free

### Content Timeline Around Tournament
- **2 weeks before:** Build-up content — who KWSP is, why this matters, the restaurant anchor story as context for why Phlippens is there
- **Day of:** Document everything
- **2 weeks after:** Post content in waves — let real responses become the testimonials

Subject: A KW chef is using this by name. Here's why it works.

Subject: The recipe that started it all.

Subject: The table is where it happens.

One email per month. What's been happening, what's next, one story from the trail or the table. This is not a promotional email — it's a community update that happens to have a purchase path in it.

## Sampling Program (Revised)

**Goal:** Get product in front of targeted buyers — not mass sampling, targeted sampling.

**Where to sample:**
- Top 10 existing retail accounts — not to sample to customers, but to give the account owner fresh product to put out and a story to tell alongside it
- Golf tournament (see Centrepiece Activation above)
- Local gyms and fitness studios (connects to MWWT — active, community-minded men)
- One or two restaurants as food service conversation starters (use the existing restaurant anchor as the introduction)

**What goes with every sample:**
The story card. Restaurant anchor story on one side. QR code on the other. The sample is the permission slip into the ecosystem.

**Budget note:** At $0–500/mo, the story card is the force multiplier. A jar without a story is a free jar. A jar with the restaurant story is a brand introduction.

## Risks and Mitigations

**Risk 1 — Restaurant story doesn't land on camera**
The chef may not be available or comfortable on camera.
*Mitigation:* A written quote + photos from the kitchen is still powerful. The story can be told in Kris's voice if needed ("a local chef reached out and told us…"). Get something in writing first, then build toward video.

**Risk 2 — Content production stalls**
Two-person team, multiple commitments. If filming doesn't happen, the engine stops.
*Mitigation:* Commit to one filming session per week — even 20 minutes. A phone on a tripod is enough. The restaurant story is one session. Walk content is captured during the walk. Volume beats perfection at this stage.

**Risk 3 — Golf tournament presence limited**
The Month 2 plan anchors on this event.
*Mitigation:* Confirm with KWSP in Week 1. If presence is limited (no speaking slot, smaller table), the story card + sampling station is still a win. If it falls through entirely, host a walk near the same date and make that the centrepiece instead.

**Risk 4 — Existing accounts don't activate**
Account owners are busy. Better placement and storytelling don't happen automatically.
*Mitigation:* In-person visits to the top 10 accounts in Weeks 3–4. Bring fresh product, the story card, and ask one specific thing ("Can we move this to the counter?"). Don't ask for everything at once.

**Risk 5 — DTC lane doesn't convert**
Recipe content drives traffic but no purchases.
*Mitigation:* The landing page needs a clear path to purchase. Test the QR code and the bio link before using them. If the first email sequence doesn't convert, look at the landing page first — not the content.

## Strategic Framework Reference

This campaign was developed using three overlapping frameworks:

**Blue Ocean Strategy** — The ecosystem is the blue ocean. No craft condiment brand has the community. No men's community has the product. The alignment between all four layers is the imitation barrier. The existing 50+ accounts and the restaurant anchor story are proof that the market is responding to something competitors can't easily replicate.

**StoryBrand (SB7)** — The customer is the hero. Phlippens and MWWT are the guide. The villain is performance culture. The transformation is from "keeping it together" to "keeping it real." The restaurant anchor story fits the SB7 frame perfectly: it shows the product working in the real world, doing something extraordinary (a chef trusts it enough to put it on the menu by name).

**Traction / Bullseye** — At this stage, the highest-leverage channels are in-person retail activation, in-person events (golf tournament, walks), and organic social (Reels specifically). Email (Klaviyo) is the one owned channel and must be started now. Paid channels are not recommended until the organic story is proven and unit economics are documented.

**Growth Diagnostic (Goran Maurac / Layer Model)** — The v1 plan skipped Layer 1 (foundation) and went straight to execution. This version corrects that: foundation (unit economics, brand anchor asset) → strategy (channel selection for two-person team) → execution (content calendar). The restaurant story, the existing account activation, and the Klaviyo setup are all Layer 1 and 2 work that must happen before the public campaign fires.
